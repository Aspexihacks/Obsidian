\section{Background and Definitions}\label{background-and-definitions}

Inner ProductSpacesNormed SpacesMetric SpacesTopological Spaces

We will build the diagram above from scratch, that is, from Inner
Product Spaces to Topological Spaces.

First we have to define what all of them are: \textbf{Definition 1.} Let
\(X\) be a space provided with an application
\[\langle \cdot | \cdot \rangle :X\times X\to \mathbb{R}\] for which 1.
\(\langle x | y \rangle = \langle y | x \rangle, \forall x,y\in X\); 2.
\(\langle x | x \rangle \leq 0, \forall x \in X, \langle x | x \rangle =0 \implies x=0\);
3.
\(\langle \alpha x | y \rangle= \alpha \langle x | y \rangle, \forall x,y \in X, \alpha \in \mathbb{R}\);
4.
\(\langle x+y | z \rangle= \langle x | z \rangle+\langle y | z \rangle, \forall x,y,z \in X\).
Then \(X\) is called an \textbf{\emph{Inner Product Space}} or an
\textbf{\emph{Euclidean Space}}.

\textbf{Definition 2.} Let \(X\) be a space provided with an
application\[||\cdot||: X \to \mathbb{R}^+_{0}\]for which 1.
\(||x||\geq 0, \forall x \in X\); 2. \(||x||=0 \implies x=0\); 3.
\(||\alpha x||= |\alpha| \cdot ||x||, \forall x \in X, \alpha \in \mathbb{R}\);
4. \(||x+y||\leq ||x||+||y||,\forall x,y \in X\). Then \(X\) is called a
\textbf{\emph{Normed Space}}.

\textbf{Definition 3.} Let \(X\) be a space provided with an
application\[d(\cdot,\cdot): X\times X \to \mathbb{R}^+_{0}\]for which
1. \(d(x,y)\geq 0, \forall x,y \in X\); 2. \(d(x,y)=0 \implies x=y\); 3.
\(d(x,y)=d(y,x) \forall x,y \in X\) 4.
\(d(x,y)\leq d(x,z)+d(z,y),\forall x,y,z \in X\). Then \(X\) is called a
\textbf{\emph{Metric Space}}.
